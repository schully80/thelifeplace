# Homepage Critique

The homepage is now much clearer than the earlier shape: the first viewport answers time, place, and action; the welcome section has a distinct spiritual role; and the Sunday, next steps, kids, FAQ, and final CTA flow is coherent.

The strongest remaining issue is mobile interruption. The cookie banner and chat control collide with the first-time guest card and bottom viewport, which weakens one of the most important first-visitor reassurance moments.

The second issue is rhythm. "What happens on Sunday" and "Next steps" now share very similar sticky-left plus card-grid behavior. That makes the page feel systematic and usable, but also slightly template-like when the two sections appear back to back. One of them should have a more distinct visual grammar, background, or density.

The first-time guest card at the top and the first-time FAQ near the bottom can both stay, but their jobs should remain different. The top card should lower anxiety before the visitor chooses an action. The FAQ should answer specific objections after the visitor has scanned the page. If the copy becomes too similar, the duplication will feel like filler.

The welcome section is doing the right strategic work. It is the spiritual climax after "Come. See. Jesus.", not a practical information block. Its larger type, quieter surrounding field, and red emphasis on Jesus and the titles are appropriate. Keep it singular and avoid adding utility links inside it.

The kids carousel now restores breadth and uses full-image containment, which fixes the cropping problem. Because it auto-advances, it should provide a pause control or less intrusive indicator treatment so motion does not become an accessibility or attention issue.

The removed map simplifies the homepage. That is acceptable because time, address, and directions appear in the hero and final CTA, but the visit page should carry the fuller "where am I going?" burden.

Recommended next moves:

1. Fix mobile overlay priority for the cookie banner and chat button.
2. Differentiate the visual rhythm between "What happens on Sunday" and "Next steps."
3. Add a pause/play affordance or calmer semantics to the kids carousel.
4. Keep the first-time card brief and emotional; keep the FAQ practical and objection-focused.
5. Preserve the welcome section as a distinct spiritual moment, not a link hub.
