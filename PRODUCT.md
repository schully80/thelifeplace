# Product Context: The Life Place

## Product Purpose

The Life Place website is the public digital front door for the church. It should help people quickly understand who the church is, where and when to visit, how to watch live, how to give, how to request prayer, and how to take a next step.

The site should reduce friction for visitors and members. Every page should feel like an invitation, not a brochure.

## Strategic Priority

1. Make first visits easy.
2. Keep Jesus central in message and emphasis.
3. Make practical actions obvious: visit, live, give, prayer, contact.
4. Build trust through real local imagery, leadership context, beliefs, and clear service details.
5. Avoid visual noise that distracts from next steps.

## Users

First-time visitors need service time, address, directions, expectations, and confidence that they are welcome.

Members need quick access to giving, live service, prayer, events, devotionals, and updates.

Explorers need beliefs, leadership, messages, and blog content to understand the church before attending.

## Product Principles

Clarity beats cleverness. Navigation labels, calls to action, and page structure should be direct.

The site should feel local and real. Prefer real photography, actual location details, and concrete copy.

Red is meaningful. Use it for primary actions, active states, live indicators, validation, and intentional Jesus emphasis.

Design should serve the invitation. Avoid decorative effects that slow people down or make the site feel generic.

## Content Rules

Use plain, welcoming language.

Prefer action labels like "Plan A Visit", "Watch Live", "Give", and "Request Prayer".

Avoid vague church marketing phrases unless they are backed by concrete next steps.

Keep theological language clear and grounded.

## Key Flows

### Visit Flow

A visitor should be able to find the service time, location, directions, and what to expect within seconds.

### Live Flow

A user should quickly know whether the church is live now, where to watch, and what happens when not live.

### Giving Flow

A giver should see trusted giving options, details, and confirmation without confusion.

### Prayer Flow

A person requesting prayer should feel privacy, care, and clarity around what happens after submission.
